Break the loop.

Four modules, one path: interrupt the loop, understand what happened, decide how to respond, and leave able to navigate it. Each module tells you exactly what to do and which tool to use, so you always know your next step.

Same thought, on repeat

Rumination is a repetitive thinking pattern. This course gives you a way through it, one module at a time.

Tools are built into the process

The dashboard connects you to the exercises and resources you will use throughout each module.

You'll see your own change

You'll take an assessment before you start and again at the end, so you can actually see how far you've come, not just take it on faith.

Lesson walkthrough

Lesson 1

Notice the Loop

Naming where you are right now, before anything else. Putting words to it, even roughly, is what breaks the automatic pull of the loop.

Lesson 2

Distract

Two forms. Immediate, to break the pattern right now, leaving the room, changing position, turning on the TV. And mindfulness through activity, giving a hobby or task your full attention, for when the loop keeps circling back after the quick version wears off.

Lesson 3

Regulate

Cognitive offloading, getting the loop out of your head and onto paper, as a strategy you can return to anytime, not a box to check once. Breathing lives outside this toolkit, in Back to Baseline, for when nothing else is available.

Lesson walkthrough

Lesson 1

Separating Fact from Story

Writing down what happened, plainly, in order, then noticing what you're assuming instead of what you actually know. When a thought keeps replaying, the facts and the story about the facts blur together. The story is usually what keeps the loop running, not the facts themselves.

Lesson 2

Why Does This Bother Me So Much?

Naming what got touched, using the SCARF model: status, certainty, autonomy, relatedness, or fairness. Then pointing to the specific moment or detail that actually did it, not just the need in the abstract.

Lesson 3

Getting to the Real Feeling

A direct "are you angry?" often gets a no, even when the answer is yes. These questions come in sideways instead, so someone can find the feeling without having to already know what it is.

Module 3 of 4

Respond

Take back your own account of the story, then decide what, if anything, you do about it.
✦

Learning Objectives

→

Tell your own voice apart from a voice you've internalized from someone else.

→

Bring feared possibilities into the open instead of leaving them vague and overwhelming.

→

Test a feared outcome against its worst case, best case, and most likely case.

→

Understand why acceptance comes first, as the foundation, not as one option among several.

→

Choose a response that actually fits the situation, instead of reacting from panic or urgency.

✦

Key Takeaways

→

Whose voice is loudest determines whether you're reclaiming your own account or still arguing with someone else's.

→

Naming a fear out loud takes away some of its power to run unexamined in the background.

→

Radical Acceptance is the starting point for everyone, not a fallback if nothing else works.

→

Repair, Record for Yourself, Build Your Defense, Boundary Setting, Escalation, and Disengagement are six real options, not one right answer.

→

You leave this module with the story in your own voice, and a real decision about what, if anything, comes next.

Lesson walkthrough

Lesson 1

Whose Voice Is Loudest

Before anything else: yours, or theirs. Naming this opens the module, because everything after it is either reclaiming your own account or still arguing with someone else's.

Lesson 2

Naming the What-Ifs

Writing out every feared possibility, as what-if statements, instead of letting them stay vague and circling.

Lesson 3

Reframe

For each what-if: the worst case, the best case, the most likely case, and how likely it really is.

Lesson 4

Radical Acceptance

The foundation everyone starts from. This happened, and it can't be changed. The feeling doesn't have to disappear for this to have worked.

Lesson 5

Choosing Your Response

What, if anything, you also do. Repair, Record for Yourself, Build Your Defense, Boundary Setting, Escalation, or Disengagement, each with its own test for whether it fits.

Difficult situations don't define who you are.

→

Every challenge is a chance to strengthen how you respond.

What this module covers

→

Reframing a setback as data about the system, not proof you're stuck.

→

Sorting what's actually yours to navigate from what isn't.

→

Gathering evidence of your own competence, not building a defense.

→

Having a plan ready before the next triggering situation, not after.

→

Outcome: you leave the course knowing you can navigate this, standing on your own account of yourself.

Lesson walkthrough

Lesson 1

The Target Belief

Defining the ending belief of the course: I can navigate this. It's uncomfortable, but it's not bigger than me.

Lesson 2

Setbacks as Information

Workplace friction is data about the system, not a verdict on your worth.

Lesson 3

What's Mine to Navigate, What Isn't

Sorting what you can actually decide, say, build, or change from what's genuinely not yours to fix.

Lesson 4

Evidence of Capability

A wins file. Competence you already have, in your own words, not a defense you're building in case of attack.

Lesson 5

The Next-Time Script

Regulation, reframe, one action, ready before you need it, so the next triggering situation doesn't start from zero.
